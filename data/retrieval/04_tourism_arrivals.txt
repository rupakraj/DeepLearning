Tourist arrivals rise but spending per visitor falls

The country received just over one million and eighty thousand foreign tourists last year, the tourism board said, the strongest figure since before the pandemic, though average spending per visitor has fallen.

India remained the largest source market, followed by China, the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia. Arrivals from Europe grew fastest in percentage terms, helped by additional flights connecting Kathmandu through Gulf hubs.

Trekking permits issued for the Annapurna and Everest regions both rose, while the Manaslu and Kanchenjunga routes recorded modest growth from a small base. Autumn remains the busiest season by a wide margin.

Hoteliers in Pokhara report high occupancy but lower room rates, with many new properties competing for the same guests. More than two thousand rooms have been added in the lakeside area in the last three years.

The regional international airport in Pokhara continues to operate with almost no international flights, three years after it opened. Officials blame the absence of bilateral flight arrangements and the limited approach path through the mountains.

Average length of stay has shortened to about eleven nights from thirteen a decade ago. Operators say visitors increasingly take shorter, road-supported treks rather than the long circuits that once defined the destination.

Guides and porters say wages have not kept pace with prices in the trekking towns, where a plate of food can cost several times what it does in the valley.

The tourism board has set a target of two million arrivals, though hotel associations argue that the target is meaningless without improvements in road access, waste management in the trekking regions and reliable domestic aviation.

Domestic tourism, largely uncounted in the official figures, is now estimated to be larger than foreign arrivals in terms of the number of trips taken within the country each year.
