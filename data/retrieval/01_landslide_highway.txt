Landslide blocks Araniko Highway for a second day

A large landslide triggered by three nights of continuous monsoon rain has blocked the Araniko Highway at Dolalghat for a second consecutive day, cutting off road access between Kathmandu and the districts to the north-east.

The slide came down at around 2 am on Sunday, burying nearly eighty metres of the carriageway under mud, boulders and uprooted trees. District police said no vehicles were caught in the debris because traffic had already been halted the previous evening as a precaution.

Around four hundred passengers travelling on night buses spent the night in Dhulikhel and Barhabise after the road was closed. Local hotels and a community school opened their halls to travellers, and the district administration arranged drinking water and a night meal.

The Department of Roads has deployed four excavators and a bulldozer, but engineers say the hillside above the highway is still moving and clearing work has to pause whenever fresh material comes down.

"We can open one lane within two days if the rain stops," the divisional engineer said. "The bigger worry is the crack we have found about sixty metres above the road. If that section collapses, we will be back to the beginning."

The Sun Koshi river below has turned muddy brown and is running well above its usual level for this time of year. Officials have asked settlements on the riverbank to stay alert through the night.

Meteorologists recorded 148 millimetres of rain in the district over twenty-four hours, one of the heaviest single-day totals of this monsoon. The forecast division has warned of more rain for the next three days across the central hills.

The highway is the main supply route for several northern municipalities, and traders say vegetable and fuel deliveries have already been delayed. Perishable goods loaded on trucks stuck at Barhabise are reported to be spoiling.

This is the fourth time this season that the same stretch of road has been closed by a slide. Engineers blame steep cuts made during road widening and the absence of proper drainage above the alignment.
