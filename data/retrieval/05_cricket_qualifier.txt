National cricket team qualifies for the World Cup after a last-over finish

The national cricket team sealed qualification for next year's Twenty20 World Cup with a four-wicket win over the United Arab Emirates in the regional final, chasing down 162 with two balls to spare.

The chase looked lost at 84 for five in the twelfth over. A stand of sixty-one between the captain and the wicketkeeper, built almost entirely in singles and twos, brought the required rate back within reach before the captain fell trying to finish it.

The winning runs came from a leg-side boundary in the nineteenth over. Supporters who had travelled to the neutral venue came onto the field, and the match was held up for several minutes.

The left-arm spinner was named player of the tournament after taking fourteen wickets in six matches at an economy rate of just above five runs an over.

Thousands gathered at the cricket ground in the capital to watch the match on a large screen, and crowds filled the ring road when the result was confirmed. The team was received at the airport the following evening.

The association has been under suspension twice in the past decade over governance disputes, and players have at times gone months without payment. The current squad trained through most of the last year on a single turf ground.

A dedicated cricket stadium in the east has been under construction for more than ten years. Officials say the outfield and drainage are complete but the pavilion and floodlights still require funding.

The team now enters a group with two full-member nations. The coach said the immediate priority is arranging practice matches abroad, as no touring side has visited in the last two seasons.

Cricket has grown quickly in popularity here, and the national league now draws crowds that fill provincial grounds, though broadcast revenue remains small.
