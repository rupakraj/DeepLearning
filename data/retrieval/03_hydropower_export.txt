Electricity export earnings cross twenty billion rupees

Nepal earned more than twenty billion rupees from electricity exports in the last fiscal year, the national utility said, the highest figure recorded since cross-border power trade began.

Exports peak during the monsoon, when run-of-river plants on the snow-fed rivers run close to full capacity and domestic demand cannot absorb the surplus. In the dry winter months the flow reverses and the country imports power to cover the evening peak.

Six hundred and ninety megawatts were approved for sale on the Indian energy exchange this year, with a smaller volume routed onward to Bangladesh under a trilateral arrangement signed last year.

The 456-megawatt plant on the Tama Koshi, the largest in the country, contributed close to a quarter of the exported volume. Three medium-sized projects in the west came online during the year and added a further two hundred megawatts.

Transmission remains the binding constraint. A single cross-border line carries most of the export volume, and engineers say a second high-capacity line is needed before any large new increase is possible.

Domestic consumption has also grown, driven by electric cooking and a rising number of electric vehicles. The utility says industrial customers now receive uninterrupted supply for the first time in more than a decade.

Investors in the sector complain that grid connection agreements are signed years after construction finishes, leaving finished plants idle and loans accumulating interest.

Analysts warn that the export figure is flattering because it hides the seasonal imbalance. Storage projects, which hold water from the monsoon for use in winter, remain few, and the largest of them is still years from completion.

The utility has set a target of exporting ten thousand megawatts within the decade, a figure that would require both a large construction programme and firm long-term purchase agreements with buyers across the border.
