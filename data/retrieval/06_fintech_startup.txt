Kathmandu payments startup raises six million dollars

A Kathmandu-based digital payments company has raised six million dollars from a Singapore investment fund, one of the largest rounds ever closed by a technology company registered in the country.

The company operates a mobile wallet used by about four million people for utility bills, mobile top-ups, school fees and small merchant payments. It says roughly a third of its users are outside the three largest cities.

Its fastest-growing product handles remittances from workers abroad, delivering money directly into the wallet instead of a bank account. Remittances are the largest single source of foreign currency for the economy.

The founders started the company in a rented room in Jhamsikhel with four engineers and now employ two hundred and forty people, most of them software developers hired from local engineering colleges.

Foreign investment in the sector has long been complicated by rules on currency conversion and profit repatriation, and the round took fourteen months to clear regulatory approval.

The central bank has tightened requirements for payment service providers, raising minimum capital and requiring that customer funds be held in a separate escrow account. Smaller wallets have struggled to meet the threshold and two have merged in the past year.

Interoperability rules introduced two years ago allow transfers between competing wallets and have cut the cost of small transfers, though merchants complain that settlement still takes a full working day.

The company says the new capital will fund merchant lending, where a shop's transaction history is used to assess a small working-capital loan, a product that requires a separate licence still awaiting approval.

Engineers here say the harder constraint is not capital but senior talent, as experienced developers are recruited quickly by firms abroad offering several times the local salary.
